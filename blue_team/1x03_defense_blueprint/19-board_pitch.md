# Task 19 — The Board Pitch

(193 words)

Four weeks of assessment found MedDefense with zero of eighteen industry-standard security controls fully in place: no multi-factor authentication, no network segmentation, and a backup system that would fail alongside production in a ransomware attack. The one-line verdict: MedDefense is defended by luck, not controls, and that luck already ran out twice this year.

If we do nothing, our single highest exposure alone, ransomware destroying production and backup together, represents an estimated $468,300 per year in expected loss, before counting patient-data breach costs that run into the millions. This is not hypothetical: our billing server was already compromised twice in the past year, once by ransomware and once by an undetected cryptominer.

We recommend $120,000 to fund six prioritized controls. The top three alone, multi-factor authentication ($4,000), isolated backup replication ($14,400), and network segmentation ($25,500), close $959,030 of that exposure by themselves. All six combined close approximately $1.14 million per year in expected loss, transforming our weakest, most exploited gaps into our strongest defenses.

For every $1 invested, MedDefense avoids roughly $9.50 in expected annual loss, a return few capital projects in this hospital can match, achieved without disrupting a single patient's care.
