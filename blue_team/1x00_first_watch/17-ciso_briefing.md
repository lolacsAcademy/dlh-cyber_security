# MedDefense — Board Briefing (300 words max)

**Opening Statement:** MedDefense's security today is high-risk: we can often stop simple attacks, but we have almost no way to detect one in progress or recover cleanly afterward.

**Critical Finding:** Our biggest problem is that every computer, server, and medical device sits on one open network, with nothing separating them. If a single employee's laptop or a single medical device is hacked, the attacker can reach patient records, hospital systems, and connected medical equipment with nothing in the way. This is the same setup that let a ransomware attack shut down a comparable hospital's ambulance service for 11 days last year.

**Three Priority Actions:**
1. Within one week: close a handful of cheap, high-risk gaps, including locking an exposed equipment closet and ending a shared login used to access patient imaging. Cost: under $6,000.
2. Within one month: separate medical devices and critical systems from the rest of the network, so one hacked laptop cannot reach patient care equipment. Cost: about $8,000.
3. Within one month: fix how we back up patient records and imaging, so one incident cannot destroy both the original data and our only backup at once. Cost: about $30,000.

**The Business Case:** The full $120,000 plan costs less than 2.5% of what a comparable hospital paid to recover from a single ransomware attack last year: over $5 million in emergency costs and lost revenue combined.

**Closing:** If the Board takes no action, we are choosing to wait for the same kind of attack that has already shut down comparable hospitals for over a week at a time.
